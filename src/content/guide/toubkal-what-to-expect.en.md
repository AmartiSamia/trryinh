At 4,167 m, Jebel Toubkal is the highest peak in North Africa. It is also one of the most accessible big mountains in the world: no ropes, no glacier, and the trailhead is less than two hours from Marrakech. That does not make it easy. Here is what the climb is really like.

## How hard is it?

Outside winter, Toubkal is a long, steep walk, not a technical climb. The path from Imlil (1,740 m) to the refuge (3,207 m) is good and well used. The summit day is the hard part: about 950 m of climbing on loose stones before sunrise, then the whole way down to Imlil in the same day.

If you can walk six hours on hilly ground with a daypack, you can usually reach the top. What stops people is rarely the legs; it is the altitude, the early start and the cold.

## Two days or three?

- **Three days** is what we recommend for most people. You sleep a night in Imlil, walk up to the refuge without rushing, and have more energy for the summit.
- **Two days** works for regular hikers who are short on time. Day one is a long climb straight to the refuge, and day two is the summit plus the full descent.

## The altitude

Most walkers feel something above 3,500 m: shorter breath, a light headache, less appetite. Walk slowly, drink a lot of water and tell your guide how you feel. Sleeping at the refuge before the summit helps your body adapt.

## When to go

- **April to June and September to October** are the best months: mild days and cold but manageable nights.
- **July and August** are fine in the mountains, though Marrakech is very hot on the way.
- **December to March**, Toubkal is under snow. It becomes a winter ascent with crampons and an ice axe, and is for experienced walkers only.

## Do you need a guide?

Yes. Since 2019, walkers in the Toubkal area must be accompanied by a licensed mountain guide. Beyond the rule, a local guide knows when the weather is turning, where the path is easy to lose, and when it is wiser to turn back.

## A typical summit day

You wake around 2:30, have breakfast at the refuge and leave at 3 with head torches. The path zigzags up the south cirque in the dark. Around sunrise you reach the summit ridge and the metal pyramid on the top. After photos and a hot tea, you walk back to the refuge for lunch, then down the valley to Imlil, and drive back to Marrakech in the evening.
